Research statement

My research combines multi-wavelength photometric and spectroscopic observations to understand single and binary stellar evolution at small scales and the evolution of dwarf galaxies at large scales.

I investigate the accretion scenario in T Tauri stars, compact-object binaries (white dwarf, neutron star and black hole with main-sequence companions), and exotic populations such as blue straggler stars. WD–MS and MS–WD binaries are key progenitors of Type Ia supernovae — used as cosmological standard candles — and of double-WD systems that are prime gravitational-wave sources for the LISA mission, directly linking stellar evolution to gravitational-wave astrophysics and observational cosmology.

I have also studied the role of environment in shaping galaxy evolution. Through studies of nearby interacting systems such as the Magellanic Clouds, I identified interaction-driven cluster-formation episodes that provide direct evidence of minor-merger processes — placing empirical constraints on near-field cosmology and hierarchical galaxy assembly.
